Lifetime:
Built to Lead

Moving a well known training provider from operational supplier to a strategic learning partner, from the first audit to a system the whole team uses.

Unanimousboard approval for the new direction
Client
Lifetime Training
Year
2025
My role
Creative lead, from audit and pitch to the brand system
Scope
Audit, positioning, three directions, system and rollout
Audience
The board, L&D and HR leaders, national employers

Aligning learningto the evolvingworkplace

Context

A trusted brand,
ready for its next chapter.

Lifetime has a strong presence and a distinctive logo, and people recognise its colours and shapes. Over the years, though, the brand had drifted, and it was still speaking mainly to apprentices at a time when the business wanted to win leaders, HR teams and new sectors.

The Lifetime logo through the years.

Challenge

Known for delivery,
ready to lead.

Competitors were building more engaging brand positions, and Lifetime was seen as an operational apprenticeship provider rather than a transformative one. Employers did not know the full range of what it offered, so specialist programmes it could deliver were going elsewhere.

The opportunity was to win back share in hospitality, adult care and retail, and to grow into professional services, early years and logistics, without losing the recognition the brand already had.

Where the brand was

  • Seen as an operations apprenticeship provider
  • Employers unaware of the full offer
  • Specialist programmes going to competitors
  • Playful look that did not reach senior buyers

Where it needed to be

  • A strategic, high value L&D partner
  • Credible with L&D and HR leaders
  • The go to provider for leadership, customer excellence, data and people development
  • Still recognisably Lifetime
Shift the perception from vanilla to a strategic, high value learning partner.

From the positioning brief

Audit

An honest look
at what was working.

I started by auditing the existing brand across colour, type, imagery, icons and layout, keeping what made it friendly and recognisable and naming what held it back with senior decision makers.

ColourPlayful and friendly, but too many colours, some of them not accessible.
TypographyClean sans serif type, used without consistency.
ImageryDiverse and people focused, but stock in feel and missing a leadership presence.
Icons and layoutToo many icons and shapes used as filler, with layouts lacking the precision leaders expect.

Directions

Three routes,
shown as the real thing.

I presented three distinct directions to the board, each with its own rationale, palette, icons and executions across web, print and social, so the decision was made on how the brand would actually look in use.

The system

Built so the team
could carry it.

The chosen direction keeps Lifetime's visual equity and strips back the noise. Every rule came from the audit, and a review process keeps quality consistent as the wider team uses it.

The lozengeOne shape, from the Lifetime leap, in place of many.
Reduced paletteDeep teal and green teal, with coral used for highlights.
Big, bold typeHighlighted headlines that lead every layout.
Led by white spaceCalmer layouts built on UX principles.
Refined iconsSimple, rounded and consistent in weight.
Cinematic photographyCandid, high contrast images with an executive presence.

In use

From web to print
to the boardroom.

The system now runs across the website, social, print and presentations, and was carried into the new Leadership and Management materials.

What it shows

Strategy, leadership
and systems thinking.

Turning an audit into a clear positioning challenge and three distinct answers to it.

Leadership

Systems thinking

Outcome

Unanimous.

Board approval for the new direction

Why it mattered: the board backed the change in full, giving Lifetime a credible brand for the leadership and professional services work it wanted to win.
